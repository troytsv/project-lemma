## Formulation and support

The ground set has fourteen block or machine indices. Nonempty incidence patterns have pairwise intersections because every pair is covered. Discard zero-weight patterns and add weights on repeated patterns. These operations preserve total mass and each load. Conversely, treat each positive pattern as a data group. A one-group distribution occupies at least one machine and has maximum load one. For literal pair coverings with at least two points, coverage already forces every point into at least one block. Isolated coordinates can be added to a system on at most fourteen vertices. An empty positive support has mass zero and cannot violate the bound.

For any fixed intersecting support the real feasible polytope at capacity 21 is compact, since each variable lies in $[0,21]$. It has a maximizing extreme point. If its positive incidence columns were dependent, a nonzero kernel vector supported on those columns would yield two distinct feasible points by taking sufficiently small steps in both directions. Their midpoint is the extreme point. Thus at most fourteen positive columns remain. This argument relies on preserving the loads, which leaves both points feasible even if some loads equal 21. It does not require that the kernel preserve the mass. The maximizer has mass at least that of the presumed counterexample.

A normalized distribution with maximum load $a<7/23$ has $a>0$. Scale its weights by $21/a$ to obtain mass $W>69$ and all loads at most 21. Conversely, if a fractional matching has total mass $M>0$ at unit capacity, normalization gives maximum load at most $1/M$. Scaling an attaining weight by the reciprocal of its maximum load gives the reverse implication. Thus the optimum values are reciprocal. For the all-to-all comparison data limit, taking incidence and taking its transpose convert data groups and patterns in both directions. These steps work for arbitrary nonnegative real weights. The rational Barker witness attains the same value for literal equal-sized items.

To compare the general weighted optimum to the unweighted covering ratio, minimize the peak load on each intersecting support using an auxiliary load variable. The constraints have rational coefficients, and the auxiliary load can be bounded between zero and one. An optimum occurs at a rational extreme point. There are finitely many supports, so one achieves the overall minimum. Clearing denominators makes each pattern a set of equally weighted copies. The block sizes may initially differ. Pad them to the maximum size by adding points already in the covering ground set, which preserves coverage. This gives the same ratio for a uniform covering with at most fourteen blocks. The reverse direction is uniform weighting. HHS Lemmas 3.1 and 3.4 give the same correspondence. [Hall, Horsley and Stinson](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10623-024-01418-6)

## Reduction from a putative counterexample

Write $t$ for the number of size-four patterns, $H$ for total weight on larger patterns, $E$ for their rank excess mass over size four, and $S$ for the sum of the load slacks. For a positive pattern $A$ of rank $r$, summing the loads on $A$ counts every weight at least once and counts $w_A$ exactly $r$ times. The relation $21r\ge W+(r-1)w_A$ gives $r\ge4$, $w_A<5$ at rank four, and $w_A<9$ at rank five. In rank four the overlap excess from other patterns is strictly less than $3(5-w_A)$ because $W>69$.

Since $S=294-4W-E\ge0$, we have $0\le H\le E<18$, total size-four weight $W-H>51$, and $11\le t\le14$. Each load is at least $21-S>3+E\ge3+H$, so every coordinate belongs to a size-four pattern. This also excludes isolated vertices in any presumed counterexample. Deficits $\delta_A=5-w_A$ are strictly positive and sum to $D=5t-W+H$.

If $D<4$, no vertex lies in five size-four patterns because they would load it by at least $25-D>21$. Their ordinary degrees $d_x$ satisfy $d_x\ge t-9$ by counting intersections with each size-four pattern through $x$. Indeed, the sum of the four degrees on that pattern is at least $t+3$ and the other three degrees total at most 12. Then $(t-9)(56-4t)\le\sum_x d_x(4-d_x)\le t(13-t)$. The first bound uses $4-d_x\ge0$. The second sums $16-(t+3)$ over the $t$ patterns. The four comparisons at $t=11,12,13,14$ are $24\le22$, $24\le12$, $16\le0$, and $0\le-14$. Therefore $D\ge4$.

For a vertex outside the larger patterns, let $d$ be its positive size-four degree. Sum the loads on the three other vertices of each incident size-four pattern. Every edge missing the vertex contributes at least once for each incident pattern. The incident edge itself contributes three copies of its own weight. This gives $63d\ge dW+(3-d)\ell_x$. For $1\le d\le3$ it gives $W\le63$. For $d=4$ it gives $4W\le252+21$, contrary to $W>69$. Thus $d\ge5$ and the incident deficits sum to $5d-\ell_x\ge4$. When there is at least one larger pattern, every size-four pattern meets it, so at most three of its deficit copies occur outside all larger patterns. Any set $O$ of outside vertices satisfies $4|O|\le3D$.

## Exhaustion of sizes and the packing contradiction

Now $5t=W-H+D>55$, hence $t\ge12$ and there are at most two larger patterns.

- With none, $D=5t-W<1$.
- With one, $t\le13$ and its weight exceeds 8. The bound on $E$ restricts its rank to five or six. In rank five its weight is below 9 by the edge inequality. In rank six its weight is below 9 by $E<18$. Thus $D=5t-W+H<5$. At least eight outside vertices would force $32\le3D$.
- With two, $t=12$ and $H=W-60+D>13$. At least one has rank five, since two larger ranks give $E\ge2H>26$. The other has weight $g>H-9>4$. If its rank is at least seven, $E\ge H+2g>21$. In rank six, $E=2H-w_Q<18$ for the five-set $Q$, which gives $H<27/2$ and $D<9/2$. Their union has size at most ten because the two patterns meet. The resulting four or more outside vertices would force $16\le3D<27/2$.

For two five-sets let $j$ be their intersection size, $O$ their complement, $m$ the number of four-sets meeting their intersection, and $D_I$ those four-sets' total deficit. Then $|O|=4+j$, $E=H$, and $D<9$. Thus $4(4+j)\le3D<27$ gives $j=1$ or $2$. Four-sets missing the intersection hit the two five-sets in separate vertices, so have at most two outside incidences. The other four-sets have at most three. Summing degrees and deficits outside gives $5|O|\le24+m$ and $4|O|\le2D+D_I$.

If $j=2$, then $D\ge8$, $H>17$, and $m\ge6$. The weight of the meeting four-sets is at least $30-D>21$. Each contributes at least once to the intersection, where the capacity for their weight is at most $2(21-H)<8$.

If $j=1$ and its vertex $u$ has slack $s_u$, then $D_I=5m-21+H+s_u$. When $m\ge3$ its load is at least $H+15-D=W-45>24$. When $m\le1$, the deficit inequality yields $20\le104-2W+3H+s_u<20-3S+s_u\le20$. The strict step uses $W>69$ and $H=294-4W-S<18-S$. This leaves $m=2$ and the other ten four-sets have total deficit $71-W-s_u<2$.

Two of those ten four-sets that met twice would give $3\delta_A+\delta_B>5$ and $3\delta_B+\delta_A>5$ from their overlap terms. Adding the bounds gives $\delta_A+\delta_B>5/2$, contrary to their total deficit. Each five-set weighs more than $20/3$ because $20\le3D$, $H=W-60+D>47/3$, and the other five-set weighs below 9. Any one of the ten four-sets has $3\delta_A<6$, so it cannot meet a five-set in two or more vertices. It hits each once and has an outside pair. Assign the four-set to its vertex in the first five-set minus $u$, giving four groups. Two four-sets of the same group already share its indexing vertex, so their outside pairs are disjoint. Five outside vertices permit at most two disjoint pairs per group. Four groups hold at most eight four-sets, contrary to ten.

## Witness and boundary cases

The split-point construction preserves intersections. An unchanged line meets each modified edge at a point other than the old split point. The two copies of the selected line share their three old points. Every other modified line contains both new points. Each old vertex lies on three unchanged lines and receives another 6 units from its lines through the old split point. Each new vertex receives $3+3\cdot6=21$. The weights sum to $9\cdot5+2\cdot3+3\cdot6=69$. Dividing by 69 gives maximum load $7/23$, and dividing by 21 gives fractional matching mass $23/7$. [Barker provenance and relabeling](../l14/prior-work.md)

A separate Sol reviewer built $\mathrm{PG}(2,3)$ from vector representatives and dot-product incidence over $\mathbb F_3$. Applying the split construction gave load 21 at all fourteen vertices. That reviewer also checked the two families by direct point replication for $n=1,2,3$. At $(70,21)$ the counts of pairs covered once, twice, three times, four times, and five times were 2107, 124, 22, 81, and 81. I independently replayed the checked-in Barker and Quantavane certificate verifiers. They covered all 2,346 and 2,415 pairs respectively, and the 70-point pair counts agreed.

The contradiction requires the strict assumption $W>69$. Some four-set weights equal 5 in the witness at $W=69$. In the covering corollary, $7v>23k$ is strict, since equality is attained at $(69,21)$. The point-copy argument uses $v\ge2$ to ensure that every point has a covering block.
