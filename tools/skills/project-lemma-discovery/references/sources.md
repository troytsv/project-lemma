# Source checks

Investigate leads across historical papers and cited-by chains, small classification boundaries and independent construction databases. Record dated URLs, precise theorem/page or entry, aliases, inclusion bounds, later papers, access limits and corrections. A search hit is a lead. Check the paper and whether its hypotheses actually imply the proposed result.

- [Covering Repository](https://www.coveringrepository.com/) and [historical La Jolla data](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24119701). Preserve point/block conventions and check upper constructions independently.
- [OEIS](https://oeis.org/), [Epoch LeanOpenProblems](https://github.com/epoch-research/LeanOpenProblems), and [published per-sample results](https://github.com/epoch-research/LeanOpenProblems-results). Reconcile informal comments, formal statement, attempts and accepted answers; check for subsequent correction.
- [Erdős Problems](https://www.erdosproblems.com/), [underlying status data](https://github.com/teorth/erdosproblems/blob/main/data/problems.yaml), and [DeepMind Formal Conjectures](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures). Track quantifiers and variants, including known disproofs.
- [Ben Green's collection](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/greenbj/papers/open-problems.pdf), [West's graph problems](https://dwest.web.illinois.edu/openp/), [MathOverflow](https://mathoverflow.net/) and original research papers. Date older lists and trace subsequent work; preserve author-proposed problems separately from new local extensions.
- [Palomar registry search](https://data.palomar-registry.org/api/v1/results) and domain-specific computational record tables. A positive registry hit warrants reading the theorem. A negative query establishes only absence of a matching indexed record.
