# Eligibility and ranking, version 1

Every candidate needs a defined domain, quantifiers and target. A gate passes only with evidence for (1) precision, (2) a credible still-unresolved objective, (3) observable success, (4) an independent verifier, and (5) a plausible route on this computer within a stated budget. A candidate can have valuable partial outcomes while a full result remains difficult. State whether the gate is for the exact problem or for a separately defined milestone. Distinguish dates of original posing from dates of a database observation.

Give each criterion an integer 0–10 and an individual evidence-based reason. Zero means adverse evidence, five means mixed or limited evidence, ten means unusually strong evidence. Unknown competition is uncertain, never a ten. The base score is the sum of score × weight / 10, out of 100.

| Criterion | Weight | Evidence to cite |
|---|---:|---|
| Tractability | 25 | Search size after reduction, positive foothold, explicit blocker |
| Confidence still open | 20 | Original source, later work, synonyms, uncertainty |
| Computational leverage | 15 | A bounded measured experiment that changes the next decision |
| Significance | 15 | Who could use the proof, obstruction, classification or method |
| Independent verification | 10 | Independent arithmetic, proof replay or certifying algorithm |
| Existing research leverage | 10 | Name the reusable lemma, code or construction |
| Broader implications | 5 | A distinct consequence, family or transfer with a mechanism |

Competition is an additional decision cost while preserving the seven requested weights. Rate headroom 0–10 from dated public work, benchmark attempts and the project's distinctive route. Deduct 0.8 × (10 − headroom) points from the base score, floored at zero. Always present both. This eight-point maximum is a transparent policy choice, not a fitted effect or probability. Check close rankings without the penalty and with plausible score changes. Repeat high-cost work only with a new distinguishing idea. Private research activity is unknowable.

Aim roughly 60% of effort at overlooked tractable targets, 30% at ambitious extensions and 10% at distinctive high-risk ideas. Apply to time rather than row counts. Treat the budget as flexible. A useful unsuccessful attempt can produce a bound, lemma, complete exclusion or reusable certified method. Define an observable stopping signal before the probe. Preserve failed approaches and compare continued investment with the next best target.
